Compounds Reviewed & Excluded

Honest Assessment — What NOT to Take
These compounds are frequently recommended in biohacking circles but fail scrutiny for different reasons. Including them here so you don't waste time or money.
Taurine (for longevity) Skip
Why skip: The 2023 Science paper (Singh et al.) claiming taurine deficiency drives aging generated massive hype. However, a 2025 Science paper (Fernandez et al.) using longitudinal data from the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging directly contradicts the core premise:
  • Taurine levels actually increase or remain stable with age in healthy humans, primates, and mice
  • No clear association between taurine levels and health measures like muscle strength
  • Gerontologist Rafael de Cabo: "no need for taurine supplementation as long as you have a healthy diet"
Verdict: If you eat meat/fish regularly, you're likely getting enough. The longevity thesis is now contested at the foundational level. Not worth the pill count.
Fadogia Agrestis Skip
Why skip: Popularized as a Tongkat Ali companion for testosterone, but the evidence doesn't hold up:
  • Almost entirely animal studies. Zero human RCTs published
  • Animal studies show dose-dependent testicular toxicity — the exact opposite of what you want
  • No standardization of active compounds across products
  • The risk/reward ratio is poor when Tongkat Ali + Boron + Ashwagandha already cover testosterone support with actual human evidence
Verdict: Hard skip. Tongkat Ali is the evidence-backed testosterone herb; Fadogia is the hype-backed one.
Dihexa (peptide) Skip
Why skip: Was widely promoted as "seven orders of magnitude more potent than BDNF" for cognitive enhancement. However:
  • The foundational 2012 paper was retracted in April 2025 after a data-fabrication investigation. The core potency claim is now unsupported
  • Dihexa activates HGF/c-Met, which is an oncogenic signaling pathway — long-term activation raises serious tumor-promotion concerns
  • Zero human trials, zero safety data
Verdict: Disqualified. The science it was built on was fabricated. Semax and Cerebrolysin are safer cognitive peptide alternatives.
Standalone Zinc Removed
Why removed: Your Life Extension Two-Per-Day already delivers 25 mg Zinc as OptiZinc (Zinc Monomethionine) — one of the most bioavailable forms — plus 2 mg Copper for mineral balance. This sits in the optimal 15–30 mg/day range.
  • The tolerable upper limit for zinc is 40 mg/day. Life Extension's standalone Zinc Caps contain 50 mg — adding them would put you at 75 mg/day, nearly double the UL
  • Chronic zinc excess depletes copper, causing anemia, neutropenia, and paradoxically weakened immunity
  • The multi already includes 2 mg copper to balance the 25 mg zinc — adding standalone zinc breaks this ratio
  • Only justified if bloodwork shows deficiency despite the multi (unlikely), or temporarily during acute illness
Verdict: Not needed. The 25 mg OptiZinc via your multivitamin is the correct dose. More zinc is actively harmful, not beneficial.

MK-677 (Ibutamoren) Tier 2

What it does: The most-studied oral GH secretagogue. Mimics ghrelin at the GHS receptor to stimulate pulsatile GH release. Key advantage: oral dosing, no injections.

Tier 2 — Brain Performance Specific

Cerebrolysin Tier 2
Porcine brain-derived peptide mixture · IV/IM · Approved in 40+ countries
What it does: A mixture of neurotrophic peptides derived from porcine brain tissue. The closest thing to a "clinical-grade" nootropic peptide — approved as a medicine (not just a research compound) in Russia, China, and most of Asia/Eastern Europe.
  • Neurotrophic cocktail: Contains fragments that mimic BDNF, CNTF, and NGF activity simultaneously
  • Clinical use: Prescribed for stroke recovery, traumatic brain injury, and dementia in 40+ countries
  • Multiple RCTs: More human trial data than any other nootropic peptide. Results show improved cognitive scores in stroke patients and some dementia subtypes
  • Neuroplasticity: Promotes synaptogenesis, dendritic branching, and neuroprotection against excitotoxicity
Limitations: A Cochrane review found mixed results, which limited Western regulatory adoption. Requires intramuscular injection (typically 5-10 mL). Not available in the US/EU as a prescribed medicine. Typical: 5-10 mL IM, daily for 10-20 days. Cycles 2-4x per year.

Best for: Maximum cognitive support. Has the most human evidence of any nootropic peptide. Particularly relevant if you want to layer a peptide on top of your Lion's Mane + Semax stack for a multi-pathway neurotrophic approach. The IM injection route is the main barrier.
PE-22-28 Tier 2

📝 Notes & Caveats
On Stack Size & Liver Load (Updated v2)
Your current stack is 10 compounds. The full proposed plan would be ~17-18 compounds. That sounds like a lot, but context matters:
  • Low liver burden additions: D3+K2 (micronutrient), Apigenin (flavonoid), Glycine (amino acid), Boron (trace mineral), CoQ10 (endogenous compound). These are not "drugs" — they're nutrients your body already uses
  • Moderate liver burden: NAC (actually supports liver detox — it's the antidote for acetaminophen overdose), Sulforaphane (induces Phase II liver detox enzymes), NMN
  • Phase additions: Still add 1-2 new compounds every 4-6 weeks. Suggested order:
    1. Weeks 1-4: D3+K2 + Apigenin + Boron (minimal, nutrient-level)
    2. Weeks 5-8: GlyNAC (Glycine + NAC) — split AM/PM
    3. Weeks 9-12: CoQ10 + Sulforaphane
    4. Weeks 13+: Tongkat Ali and/or NMN+TMG (optional, assess need)
  • Get baseline bloodwork before starting. Repeat at week 12
Interaction Notes (Updated v2)
  • Ashwagandha + Tongkat Ali: Both affect HPA axis. Don't double up continuously. Alternate or cycle them
  • ALA + NMN: Both affect NAD+ metabolism indirectly. No known negative interaction, potentially synergistic
  • Apigenin + Ashwagandha + Glycine: All mildly sedating. This is a feature for the bedtime slot — your sleep stack becomes very strong
  • NAC + ALA: Both antioxidants that recycle glutathione. Synergistic, no conflict. NAC in the morning, ALA before first meal — they can go together
  • CoQ10 + NMN: Complementary mitochondrial support. CoQ10 carries electrons in the ETC, NMN fuels NAD+ for Complex I. No conflict
  • Sulforaphane + NAC: Both upregulate glutathione production through different pathways (Nrf2 vs. direct precursor). Synergistic for detoxification
  • Boron + Tongkat Ali + Ashwagandha: All support free testosterone through different mechanisms (SHBG reduction, LH stimulation, cortisol reduction). Together they form a strong natural T-optimization triad
  • Creatine + everything: No known negative interactions. Can be taken at any time. Moved to morning for simplicity
  • L-Citrulline + Creatine: Popular pre-workout combo. Can be taken together if you train in the morning
  • MK-677 + Glycine: Both taken at night, both improve sleep quality. MK-677 increases deep sleep stages; Glycine lowers core body temp for sleep onset. Potentially excellent synergy for recovery
  • MK-677 + ALA: Important interaction — MK-677 can impair insulin sensitivity. ALA helps counteract this. If running MK-677, keep ALA in the stack
On Peptide Sourcing
This is the single most important factor in peptide use. The supplement market has quality issues; the peptide market has quality crises.
  • Only buy from vendors providing third-party HPLC + mass spectrometry CoA (not just vendor-provided CoA)
  • Purity must be ≥98% (ideally ≥99%)
  • Reputable sources frequently discussed: Peptide Sciences, Swiss Chems, Limitless Life Nootropics (for Semax/Selank nasal sprays)
  • If reconstituting lyophilized peptides yourself, use bacteriostatic water (BAC water), not sterile water, for multi-use vials
  • Store reconstituted peptides refrigerated (2-8°C). Most are stable for 4-6 weeks once reconstituted
